Glossary

Plain-language definitions of terms you will meet when choosing design software.

BIM
Building Information Modelling: a 3D model where walls, doors and furniture carry data (size, material, cost), so plans, sections and schedules update from one model.
CAD
Computer-aided design: drawing software for precise 2D plans and 3D geometry, such as AutoCAD.
Floor plan
A top-down drawing of a room or building showing walls, openings and furniture to scale.
Elevation
A drawing of a wall seen straight-on, used to show heights, built-ins and finishes.
Section
A drawing that cuts through a space to show vertical relationships, such as ceiling heights and levels.
Mood board
A collage of images, colours and materials that communicates the style of a project before detailed design.
FF&E
Furniture, fixtures and equipment: the movable and non-structural items specified in a project.
Render
A realistic image generated from a 3D model with lighting and materials applied.
Real-time rendering
Rendering that updates instantly as you move the camera or change the model, as in Enscape, Twinmotion or D5 Render.
Ray tracing
A rendering method that simulates how light bounces between surfaces to produce realistic shadows and reflections.
Walkthrough
An animated or interactive camera path through a 3D model, used to present a space to a client.
Texture
An image applied to a 3D surface to imitate a material, such as wood, stone or fabric.
PBR material
Physically based rendering material: a set of maps (colour, roughness, normal) that describe how a surface reacts to light.
IFC
An open file format for exchanging BIM data between different programs.
DWG
The native drawing file format of AutoCAD, widely used to exchange 2D plans with architects and contractors.
LiDAR
A sensor that measures distance with laser pulses; used by some phones and scanners to capture room dimensions.
Point cloud
A set of 3D points captured by a scanner that represents the shape of a real space.
Punch list
A list of small defects or unfinished items to fix before a project is handed over.
Scope of work
A written description of what the designer will and will not deliver in a project.
White-label
Software that a business can present under its own brand name and domain.
