Interior Design Project Workflow: From Brief to Handover

Published 2026-09-24 · Facts checked 2026-10-15 · By the DesignTested team

Most project problems come from unclear stages: work starts before the brief is agreed, or changes arrive after ordering. A defined workflow gives you checkpoints. This guide sets out a common seven-stage structure. Adjust names and depth to your practice; this is general professional advice.

Stage 1: Enquiry and brief

Goal: confirm the project is a fit and capture what the client wants.

  • Ask about scope (which rooms, what work), budget range, deadline, and who decides.
  • Run a structured brief: how they use the space, what is not working, styles they like and dislike, special requirements (children, pets, accessibility).
  • Confirm your fee approach and what is included.

Deliverable: written brief and a proposal or letter of engagement. See the quote and budget guide.

Stage 2: Survey and measure

Goal: reliable base drawings.

  • Measure every room, including ceiling heights, window and door positions, and fixed services.
  • Photograph each wall and note existing conditions.
  • Record anything you could not verify.

Follow how to measure a room for a floor plan. Tools such as laser measurers and room scanners can speed this up, but check critical dimensions by hand.

Stage 3: Concept

Goal: client approves a direction.

Produce layout options, a moodboard and key materials. Present using the structure in how to present a design concept.

Approval gate: written sign-off on layout and direction.

Stage 4: Design development

Goal: turn the concept into specific, priced decisions.

  • Finalise the layout and elevations.
  • Select furniture, lighting, finishes and fittings, with sizes, finishes and lead times, held in a material and furniture library.
  • Coordinate with electricians, joiners and other trades where relevant.
  • Prepare a detailed budget.

Approval gate: signed-off selections and budget before anything is ordered.

Stage 5: Documentation

Goal: everyone building or supplying works from the same information.

  • Dimensioned plans, elevations, lighting and electrical points, and finish schedules.
  • Furniture and fittings schedule with product codes.
  • Version numbers and dates on every drawing.

Drafting tools are covered in 3D modelling and drafting software.

Stage 6: Procurement and site

Goal: items arrive correctly and works follow the drawings.

  • Place orders only from the approved schedule. Keep order confirmations, lead times and deposits in one tracker.
  • Check deliveries against the order on arrival; note damage immediately.
  • Visit site at agreed points and record decisions made on the day, in writing.
  • Log variations (changes to scope) with their cost and time effect before they proceed.

Stage 7: Styling, snagging and handover

Goal: the client receives a finished, working space.

  • Install and style; walk the space with a snag list.
  • Track defects to resolution with the supplier or contractor responsible.
  • Hand over care instructions, warranties and supplier contacts.
  • Request feedback and, if the client agrees, photography.

Stage summary

Stage Main output Gate
Brief Written brief, engagement terms Signed agreement
Survey Measured base plans Plans checked
Concept Layout, moodboard Direction approved
Development Selections, budget Selections and budget approved
Documentation Drawings, schedules Issued for ordering/works
Procurement and site Orders, site records Deliveries and works accepted
Handover Snag list closed, documents Client acceptance

Common mistakes

  • Starting design before the brief and fee are agreed.
  • Treating verbal approvals as final. Get them in writing.
  • Ordering before selections and budget are signed off.
  • No variation log, so cost creep goes unnoticed.
  • Skipping the snag walk and closing the project informally.

Checklist: before closing a project

  • All variations recorded and invoiced.
  • Snag list cleared.
  • Warranties and care documents given to the client.
  • Final account issued.
  • Project files archived and library updated with what you learned.
