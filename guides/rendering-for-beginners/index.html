Rendering for Interior Designers: What to Know Before You Buy Software

Published 2026-09-29 · Facts checked 2026-10-13 · By the DesignTested team

A render is a realistic image generated from a 3D model, with materials, lighting and furniture applied. Clients understand renders faster than plans, so many designers want them. But rendering software is sold in very different shapes, and buying the wrong one is an expensive mistake. Here is what to know first. For a ranked list, see Best Rendering Software for Interior Designers.

Rendering needs a model first

A renderer does not design the room. It needs a 3D model with walls, openings and furniture. That model comes from either an all-in-one design tool or a separate modelling program. Your first decision is which of these you want.

Three kinds of tools

All-in-one design tools. You plan, furnish and render in one product. Foyr Neo combines 2D planning, 3D modelling and rendering, with HD, 2K and 4K renders on every plan. Planner 5D lists unlimited watermark-free 4K renders on its Professional plan. Coohom describes cloud rendering with no software to install, and Homestyler offers unlimited 1K renders on its free plan, with 2K and 4K on paid plans or per render.

Real-time renderers. These sit on top of a model you build elsewhere and show changes as you work.

  • Enscape works inside Revit, SketchUp, Rhino, Archicad and Vectorworks.
  • Lumion takes models from CAD or BIM tools through its LiveSync plugin and lists SketchUp and Revit among its integrations.
  • D5 Render lists support for SketchUp, Revit, Rhino, 3ds Max, Cinema 4D, Archicad, Blender and Vectorworks.

General 3D suites. Blender is free and open source and can model, light and render, but it is not interior-specific. You build the workflow yourself. SketchUp is a general modelling tool that often serves as the source model for the real-time renderers above.

What it costs

Pricing models differ as much as the tools. Figures below are from the vendors’ pages as read on 12 October 2026.

  • Credits per month: Foyr Neo Basic is $39/month with 30 render credits; Standard is $89/month with 180; Premium is $159/month with unlimited.
  • Per render: Homestyler lists pay-as-you-go renders, for example a 2K image at $0.99 and a 4K image at $1.99. RoomSketcher lists $4 per render as a pay-per-item option.
  • Annual subscription: Enscape Solo is $47.90/month billed annually ($574.80). Lumion Pro, named user, is $96/month billed upfront for a year ($1,149).
  • Free tiers: D5 Render has a free Community plan, but it is for non-commercial use; commercial work requires Pro. Blender is free for any purpose.

Remember that a renderer may be only part of your cost. If it plugs into SketchUp, you also need a SketchUp plan, listed from $10.75/month (Go) to $33.25/month (Pro), billed annually.

Be honest about the learning curve

An all-in-one tool is usually the fastest route to a first image. A plugin renderer is faster per revision once your model is good, but modelling takes time to learn. A general suite like Blender gives the most control and asks the most of you. We have not hands-on tested these tools, so use the free trials (Enscape, Lumion and Foyr Neo list them) before paying.

What clients actually need

Many projects do not need photoreal renders at all. A clear furnished floor plan, a mood board and a few 3D views can win approval. Save rendering for the spaces where a client cannot imagine the result, such as a full renovation or custom joinery. Tools like RoomSketcher cover plans and simple visuals at a lower cost.

Checklist

  • I know whether I want one all-in-one tool or a renderer plus a modeller.
  • I know how many renders per month I realistically need.
  • I checked whether the plan I need allows commercial use and removes watermarks.
  • I tested the free trial or free plan with one of my own rooms.
  • I counted the cost of the host modelling software too.
  • My computer meets the vendor’s stated requirements.
