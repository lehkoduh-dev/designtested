Blender Review 2026: Free Open-Source 3D Suite, Not Built for Interiors Specifically

Last updated 2026-10-12 · By the DesignTested team

Quick take

Best for: Artists and designers comfortable modelling, lighting and rendering their own scenes who want a free tool with no licence fees, including for commercial work.

Not ideal if: Designers who want a ready-made room planner, furniture catalogue or client-presentation workflow, since the vendor's pages do not position Blender as interior-design software.

Our verdict: Blender's published terms make it a zero-cost, commercially usable 3D suite with an active release schedule. Its general-purpose scope means interior-specific workflows would have to be built by the user, which we have not evaluated.

Based on the vendor’s published information; hands-on notes will be added.

What it is

Blender is a free and open-source 3D creation suite coordinated by the Blender Foundation. Its stated mission is to put “the world’s best 3D CG technology in the hands of artists as free/open source software.” The current release on the download page is Blender 5.2.2 LTS (released 15 September 2026), with Blender 5.3 listed as upcoming.

Who it’s for

Blender is a general-purpose 3D tool. Nothing on the vendor’s pages positions it as interior-design software, so it suits designers who are willing to model, light and render scenes themselves rather than work from a ready-made room planner.

Key features

  • Free for any purpose, including commercial work; the Foundation states that what you create with Blender, including images, movies and .blend files, is yours to use as you like.
  • Released under the GNU General Public License; the Cycles rendering engine is available under Apache 2.0.
  • The 5.2 release is described as having node-powered physics, online asset libraries and “fierce new features throughout,” with two years of LTS updates.
  • Installers for Windows (including Windows on Arm), macOS (Apple Silicon, macOS 13 or newer) and Linux, plus Steam and Microsoft Store versions.

Pricing

Free. Blender’s main income comes from Development Fund memberships and donations, not from licence fees (blender.org, read 12 October 2026). The Blender FAQ (blender.org/support/faq, read 13 October 2026) confirms the software can be downloaded free of charge.

Pros

  • No licence cost and no per-seat fees.
  • Open source with an active release schedule and long-term-support versions.
  • Works on the three main desktop operating systems.

Cons

  • Not tailored to interior design on the pages we read; there is no mention of room planners, furniture catalogues or client-presentation tools.
  • Interior-specific workflows would have to be built from add-ons, asset libraries or your own modelling, which we have not evaluated.

Alternatives

If you want purpose-built interior tools, see SketchUp, Foyr Neo or Coohom. For a free planner, see Sweet Home 3D.

FAQ

Can I use Blender commercially? Yes. Blender’s FAQ (read 13 October 2026) states that any creation you make with Blender is your sole property and can be used for any purpose, which also applies to add-ons and Python scripts you write.

Does Blender run on my computer? The FAQ says versions are available for Windows, macOS and Linux, and notes that a workstation certified for 3D graphics is always best. A full requirements list is published on blender.org.

Is the .blend file mine? Per the FAQ, the output of Blender in the form of .blend files is considered program output and is the sole copyright of the user.
