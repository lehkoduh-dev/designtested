Interior visualisation needs furniture, fixtures, materials and lighting. Online libraries supply them: 3D models, PBR textures and HDRI skies that drop into SketchUp, Blender, D5 and similar tools. What differs most between libraries is the licence, so we focus on what each one publishes about it.

## How we chose

We picked five libraries that publish their terms on their own websites, read on 2026-10-15. Licence and price information below is quoted or summarised from those pages only. We have not hands-on tested any of the libraries. Licence terms can change and can differ per asset, so check the licence shown on the asset page before using it in client work. Poliigon is not included because its pricing page was blocked when we fetched it.

| Library | Content | Licence as published | Price as published |
|---|---|---|---|
| Poly Haven | HDRIs, textures, 3D models | CC0 | Assets free; support via Patreon |
| ambientCG | PBR materials, HDRIs, models | CC0 1.0 Universal | Assets free; support via Patreon |
| Blendkit (formerly BlenderKit) | Models, materials, HDRIs, scenes, brushes | Royalty Free or CC0, per asset | Free plan $0/month; Full plan $9.90/month |
| Sketchfab | 3D models | Standard or editorial licence for purchased assets | Not published on the page read |
| 3D Warehouse | Pre-made 3D models for SketchUp | Not published on the page read | Not published |

## 1. Poly Haven

**Our pick.** It publishes the simplest licence in this roundup: every asset is CC0, with no credit needed, and the page says use is allowed for any purpose, including commercial work. That removes the per-asset licence check for client projects. This is a judgement from the licence page, not from testing the assets, and Poly Haven's catalogue is not interior-specific.

Poly Haven states that all its HDRIs, textures and 3D models are the original work of its staff or of artists who donate or sell their work to it, and are licensed CC0. The licence page also contains website Terms of Service, for example against web scraping without permission. It asks frequent commercial users to consider supporting it on Patreon.

## 2. ambientCG

ambientCG says all its assets are provided under the Creative Commons CC0 1.0 Universal licence, covering the downloadable files and the preview renders. According to the licence page you can copy, modify and distribute them, including for commercial purposes, without asking permission. Credit is not required but appreciated.

The home page describes the site as a source of free PBR materials, HDRIs and models for 3D art, architectural visualisation, game development and VFX, and says it offers 2000+ PBR materials, HDRIs and more. Patreon perks include ad-free browsing and early access.

## 3. Blendkit (formerly BlenderKit)

Blendkit describes itself as the largest asset library integrated into Blender; it also lists integrations for Godot, Rhino and Maya. Its pricing page shows a free plan at $0/month (23,723 models, 42,368 materials, 5,089 HDRIs) and a Full plan at $9.90/month that unlocks the entire database (82,645 models). A Business plan is listed at $29/month per seat for companies with more than 5 employees or yearly revenue above $100k. The page mentions a 50% student discount.

The licences page says there are two licences: Royalty Free, which allows commercial use without credit but not re-selling the asset in the same form, and CC0. It states that everything you download is available for commercial use. Counts and prices are as shown on 2026-10-15 and change as the library grows.

[Blendkit plans](https://www.blenderkit.com/pricing/) · [Licences](https://www.blenderkit.com/docs/licenses/)

## 4. Sketchfab

Sketchfab describes itself as a platform to publish, share and discover 3D content, with a category for Furniture & Home among others. Its licence page explains how purchased royalty-free models are licensed: there is a standard licence and an editorial licence, and editorial assets cannot be used for commercial or promotional purposes.

Even under the standard licence the page forbids, among other things, passing the asset on as a stand-alone file or selling it, and using it to promote alcohol, tobacco, gambling or weapons. Free downloads and their licences are set per model and not summarised on the page we read, and no price list is given there, so check each model's page.

[Sketchfab licence agreement](https://sketchfab.com/licenses)

## 5. 3D Warehouse

3D Warehouse describes itself as a website of searchable, pre-made 3D models that works seamlessly with SketchUp, which makes it a natural source of furniture and fixtures for SketchUp users. The site page itself needs JavaScript and showed no licence or price terms in our fetch, so both are listed as not published here.

Models on a shared library are uploaded by many people, and their terms can differ, so read the terms on the site before using a model in a paid project.

[3D Warehouse](https://3dwarehouse.sketchup.com/)
