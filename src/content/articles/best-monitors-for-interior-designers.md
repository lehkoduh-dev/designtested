When you show a client a fabric, a paint colour or a rendered living room, the screen is part of the proposal. A monitor that shifts greens towards yellow or shows a wide gamut as oversaturated can make a sample look different from the real thing, and a render that looked right on your desk can disappoint on site. A colour-accurate display with wide coverage and a way to calibrate it keeps what you approve on screen closer to what gets ordered and built.

## How we chose

This list is based on specifications published by each manufacturer on its own website. We have not tested these monitors hands-on, and we do not rate or rank them. We looked for current models with a wide colour gamut (sRGB, Adobe RGB and/or DCI-P3 coverage), a resolution suited to detailed renders, and a stated route to calibration. Prices change often and vary by region, so we list a price only where the official page shows one; none of the pages we used did.

**Our pick: BenQ SW272U.** Of the 27-inch 4K models here, it publishes the tightest accuracy figure (Delta E ≤ 1.5) alongside 99% Adobe RGB and 99% P3 coverage, and it supports hardware calibration. That combination suits designers who work between print samples, screen presentations and renders. This is a judgement from the published specs, not a test result. The ASUS PA279CRV is very close on paper if you prefer its port selection.

## 1. BenQ SW272U

BenQ lists the SW272U as a 27-inch 4K (3840 x 2160) monitor covering 100% sRGB, 99% Adobe RGB and 99% P3, with a Delta E of 1.5 or less. It uses a 16-bit 3D lookup table, supports hardware calibration through BenQ's AQCOLOR software, and has a Nano Matte panel to cut reflections. A USB-C port provides 90W of power for a laptop.

## 2. ASUS ProArt Display PA279CRV

ASUS describes the PA279CRV as a 27-inch 4K IPS monitor with 100% sRGB, 99% Adobe RGB and 99% DCI-P3 coverage. It is Calman Verified and factory pre-calibrated to Delta E < 2. Inputs include USB-C (DisplayPort over USB-C with 96W power delivery), two DisplayPort 1.4 and two HDMI 2.0 ports, plus a USB hub.

## 3. Eizo ColorEdge CS2740

Eizo lists the CS2740 as a 27-inch 4K UHD IPS hardware-calibration monitor with a typical Adobe RGB coverage of 99%. It offers USB Type-C, DisplayPort and HDMI inputs, a Digital Uniformity Equalizer, 10-bit display from a 16-bit lookup table, and a 5-year manufacturer's warranty. Calibration is handled by Eizo's ColorNavigator 7 software.

## 4. Apple Studio Display

Apple's specifications list a 27-inch 5K Retina display (5120 x 2880, 218 pixels per inch) with wide colour (P3), 600 nits brightness and support for one billion colours. It includes reference modes such as Design and Print (P3-D50), Photography (P3-D65) and Internet and Web (sRGB). Connections are two Thunderbolt 5 ports and two USB-C ports, with 96W host charging. Apple does not state sRGB or P3 percentages or a Delta E figure on the specs page.

## 5. ASUS ProArt Display PA32UCXR

ASUS lists the PA32UCXR as a 32-inch 4K IPS monitor with a mini-LED backlight (2,304 local dimming zones), 100% sRGB, 99% Adobe RGB and 97% DCI-P3 coverage, and peak HDR brightness of 1,600 cd/m². It has a built-in motorised colorimeter for self-calibration, supports Dolby Vision, HDR10 and HLG, and offers two Thunderbolt 4 ports with 90W USB-C power delivery. The larger panel and HDR focus make it more relevant to rendering and video work than to a basic design desk.
